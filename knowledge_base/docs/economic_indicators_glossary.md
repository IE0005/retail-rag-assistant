# Economic Indicators Glossary

Four regional economic indicators are tracked alongside weekly sales,
because consumer demand responds to the broader economic environment, not
just calendar effects.

## CPI (Consumer Price Index)

A measure of the general price level in the store's region. Rising CPI
(inflation) tends to correlate with higher nominal Weekly_Sales figures
even when unit volume is flat, since the same basket of goods costs more
dollars. When interpreting a sales trend, a rising CPI period should raise
the bar for what counts as "real" growth versus price-driven growth.

## Unemployment

Regional unemployment rate. Higher unemployment generally depresses
discretionary spending first, with essentials categories proving more
resilient. A store in a region with rising unemployment may show demand
softening concentrated in general merchandise departments while grocery
and essentials hold steady — a pattern worth checking before assuming a
sales decline is store-specific rather than regional-economic.

## Fuel_Price

Regional average fuel price. Affects both transportation-sensitive
categories and overall discretionary spending, since higher fuel costs
reduce the share of household budget available for other purchases. Also
correlates with shopping frequency — some demand research suggests
customers consolidate trips when fuel prices rise, which can show up as
fewer, larger basket sizes rather than a pure volume drop.

## Temperature

Average regional temperature for the week. Primarily relevant for
seasonal and weather-sensitive categories (apparel, outdoor goods,
seasonal food items). A department showing an unexplained sales swing is
worth checking against Temperature before assuming it reflects a real
demand shift, especially for departments known to be weather-sensitive.

## Using these indicators together

No single indicator should be read in isolation. A week with rising CPI,
flat unemployment, and seasonally normal temperature showing a sales
increase is a different story than the same sales increase occurring
alongside falling unemployment — the first looks like inflation-driven
nominal growth, the second looks like genuine demand growth.
